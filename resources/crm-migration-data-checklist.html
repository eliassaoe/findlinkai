CRM Migration Data Checklist: Map Fields, Keep Relationships, Test the Import

The data side of moving between CRMs: decide what moves, clean it first, map every field and picklist, keep companies, contacts and deals linked, and test with 50 rows before the full import.

A CRM migration rarely fails on the day of the import. It fails in the weeks after, when a rep opens an account and its contacts are missing, a deal has lost its company, or half the lead statuses read "Other".

This checklist covers the data side of moving between CRMs: what to move, how to map fields and picklists, how to keep records linked to each other, and how to test before the full import. It applies whether you are moving from Salesforce to HubSpot, from Pipedrive to Zoho, or from spreadsheets into your first CRM.

Before you start: back up the old CRM

Take a full export of every object, with record IDs, and store it somewhere no import tool can reach. Name it with the date. If something goes wrong during the migration, this file is how you recover it, and it is your proof of what existed before.

Also check the old contract: some CRMs limit exports or delete data soon after the subscription ends, so export before you give notice.

1

Inventory what you have

List every object the old CRM holds and how many records each has: companies (accounts), contacts, deals (opportunities), activities, notes, attachments, products, custom objects. Write the counts down. They are the numbers you will check against after the import.

Then decide what moves. Years of logged emails and closed-lost deals from 2019 rarely earn their place in a new system. A common cut: all companies and contacts, open deals plus closed deals from the last 24 months, and activity history attached only as a note or an archive file.

2

Clean before you move, not after

Every duplicate and dead email you migrate becomes a problem in two systems instead of one. Clean in the old CRM, where the merge tools and the history already exist:

  • Merge duplicates on email for contacts and on domain for companies. Keep the oldest record so its ID and history survive.
  • Verify emails and mark hard bounces, so the new CRM's sending reputation does not start with a bounce spike.
  • Archive the dead weight: contacts with no activity in years and no open deal can go to an archive export instead of the new system.

If the list is long, our guide to cleaning stale CRM contact records walks through this part in detail.

3

Map every field and picklist

Build a field map in a spreadsheet before anything is imported. One row per old field, with these columns:

  • Old field and its type (text, number, date, picklist, multi-select, user, lookup).
  • New field, its type, and whether it already exists or must be created first.
  • Transformation, if any: split a full name, convert a date format, translate a picklist value.
  • Decision: migrate, merge into another field, or drop.

Picklists need their own table. "Lead status = Working" in the old system may be "In progress" in the new one, and a value with no match will either be rejected or silently created as a new option. Map every value, including the ones only a handful of records use. Do the same for owners: old user names must match a user who exists in the new CRM, or records arrive unassigned.

4

Keep the record relationships

This is where most migrations lose data. A contact without its company, or a deal without its contact, is technically imported and practically useless.

  • Export the old IDs of every record and every lookup: the contact's company ID, the deal's company and contact IDs.
  • Create a "Legacy ID" field on each object in the new CRM and import the old ID into it. It is the key you will join on, and it lets you trace any record back later.
  • Import in dependency order: users first, then companies, then contacts (linked to companies by legacy company ID or domain), then deals (linked to both), then activities and notes.
  • Check many-to-many links. A contact attached to two companies, or a deal with three contacts, needs an association file, not a single column.
5

Normalise formats for the new system

The new CRM will validate data the old one accepted. Fix it in the file, not after a failed import:

  • Dates in one format (ISO 2026-11-27 is the safest), with time zones decided for datetime fields.
  • Phone numbers in international format (+33 6 12 34 56 78), country code included.
  • Countries and states as the new CRM expects them, often a fixed list or ISO codes.
  • Domains stripped of https://, www. and trailing paths, since many CRMs dedupe companies on domain.
  • Currencies and amounts as plain numbers, with the currency in its own column.
  • Required fields filled: if the new CRM requires a last name or a deal stage, decide the default for rows that lack one.
6

Test with 50 rows per object

Pick 50 records per object that cover the hard cases: a contact with two companies, a deal with every field filled, accented names, empty picklists, a very old record. Import them into the new CRM (a sandbox if you have one) and check by eye:

  • Every mapped field landed in the right place, with the right type.
  • Picklist values match, and no unexpected new values were created.
  • Contacts show their company; deals show their company and contacts.
  • Owners are assigned; dates did not shift by a day because of a time zone.
  • Re-importing the same file updates the records instead of creating duplicates.

Fix the map, delete the test records, and repeat until a test run needs no correction.

7

Run the full import and reconcile the counts

Freeze changes in the old CRM for the migration window, or export a delta of what changed during it. Run the imports in the same dependency order as the test, then compare:

  • Record counts per object against the inventory from step 1, minus what you chose to archive.
  • Relationship counts: contacts with a company, deals with a contact, before and after.
  • Totals that matter to the business: open pipeline value, deals per stage, contacts per owner.

Read the import error file line by line. Every rejected row is a record that will be missing, and the reason is usually a picklist value or a required field you can fix and re-import.

8

Check, then switch off the old CRM

Before anyone loses access to the old system:

  • Spot-check 20 random records side by side in both systems.
  • Have each sales rep open their own top 10 accounts and confirm nothing is missing.
  • Rebuild the integrations (forms, email, calendar, enrichment) and check that new records arrive with the right fields.
  • Keep the full export from step 1 and the old CRM in read-only mode for at least one sales cycle.

The checklist

  • Full export saved, record counts per object written down
  • Scope decided: what moves, what is archived
  • Duplicates merged and emails verified in the old CRM
  • Field map written, every picklist value and owner mapped
  • Legacy ID field created on every object
  • Import order set: users, companies, contacts, deals, activities
  • Dates, phones, countries, domains and amounts normalised
  • 50-row test per object checked by eye, re-import updates instead of duplicating
  • Full import run, counts and relationships reconciled, error file cleared
  • Integrations rebuilt, old CRM read-only for one sales cycle
