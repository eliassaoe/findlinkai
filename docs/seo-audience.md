Caveat worth stating: 21 payments across the whole period is a thin base, so the
paid column is directional. Visitor, signup and pricing-exposure columns are
solid, and the argument below rests mostly on those.

## 1. The Instagram cluster is a fifth of your traffic and none of your revenue

`/instagram-profile-url-finder` is the **single biggest entry point on the site**
— 5,245 people, more than the homepage. It is 43% low-conversion geography, it
converts at 1.6% to signup against the homepage's 13%, two people out of 5,245
ever saw a pricing page, and nobody has ever paid.

Add `/instagram-profile-scraper` and it is ~5,800 visitors producing zero revenue.

This is not a bad page. It ranks, and it works. It is just answering a question
asked by people who are not buying B2B contact data — Instagram lookups are a
creator and consumer query, and your product is LinkedIn enrichment for sales.

**Do not change what ranks.** Those head terms are earning the traffic. Change
what happens after the click:

- Add a qualifier to the title that keeps the head term and filters the click:
  `Instagram Profile URL Finder — Free Tool for B2B Lead Research`. Consumers
  skip it, B2B researchers self-select in. Slightly lower CTR, materially better
  audience.
- Make the hero say what the product is for, above the tool, not below it.
- Point the post-result CTA at the LinkedIn workflow rather than a generic signup.

If after a month it still produces nothing, the honest answer is that it is a
brand-awareness page, and it should stop being optimised for conversion at all.

## 2. The free win: two near-identical pages, one converts

`/linkedin-email-finder` and `/linkedin-search-by-email` are the same shape —
~3.6K visitors each, ~50% low tier, ~220 signups each. But:

| | signups | saw pricing | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| /linkedin-email-finder | 233 | 22 | **9.4%** |
| /linkedin-search-by-email | 215 | 52 | **24.2%** |

Two and a half times the pricing exposure from equivalent traffic, and 4 payments
against 0. Something on the search-by-email path pushes people toward pricing and
the email-finder path does not. Find it and copy it — that is a free doubling of
pricing exposure on 3,767 visitors, with no new traffic and no ranking risk.

Worth diffing the two pages before anything else on this list.

## 3. Pages that bring readers, not users

`/best-proxy-providers` (208 visitors, 0 signups),
`/linkedin-company-employees-finder` (148, 0), `/linkedin-company-post-scraper`
(84, 0), `/best-free-lead-generation-tools` (53, 0).

These pull people researching a topic rather than looking for a tool. They are
not harmful, but they should not get further investment, and their internal links
should push toward the pages that do convert.

## What this says about the top of the funnel

The homepage converts visitors to signup at **13%** and produced 5 of 21 payments
from 3,806 people. Every tool page is between 0.6% and 6%. The tool pages bring
five times the traffic and a fraction of the intent.

That is the shape of the whole problem: **the traffic is real and the intent is
thin**. Nothing in the app fixes that — it has to be fixed at the door, either by
qualifying harder on the tool pages, or by getting more of the traffic that
behaves like homepage traffic.
